Mount Everest

The highest point on Earth above sea level, reaching 8,848.86 meters (29,031.7 ft) in the Mahalangur Himal sub-range of the Himalayas. Straddling the border of Nepal and China, it attracts hundreds of elite mountaineers every year despite extreme risks like thin air, high winds, and avalanche hazards.

Mount Fuji

Japan's highest peak at 3,776 meters (12,389 ft), Mount Fuji is an active stratovolcano that last erupted in 1707. Famous for its remarkably symmetrical snow-capped cone, it is one of Japan's "Three Holy Mountains," an enduring cultural icon in Japanese art, and a popular pilgrimage and hiking destination.

Eiffel Tower

Constructed by Gustave Eiffel's company for the 1889 World's Fair, this 330-meter (1,083 ft) wrought-iron lattice tower was initially criticized by Paris's artistic elite. Today, it stands as one of the most recognizable structures in the world and the most-visited paid monument globally.

The Last Blockbuster

Located in Bend, Oregon, this franchise location became famous as the very last operating Blockbuster Video store on Earth after the corporate chain went bankrupt. It operates both as a fully functioning movie rental store and a pop-culture nostalgia museum, drawing tourists from around the globe.

The Pyramids of Giza

Built during Egypt's Old Kingdom around 2600–2500 BCE as monumental tombs for Pharaohs Khufu, Khafre, and Menkaure. The Great Pyramid of Khufu is the oldest and largest of the complex and stands as the only surviving wonder of the original Seven Wonders of the Ancient World.
